RailVue AI — Presentation & Defense Guide

Real-Time Dynamic ETA Prediction System for Indian Railways

Core Project Idea in Simple English: Traditional railway apps show Scheduled Time + Current Delay. If a train is 20 minutes late, the app blindly assumes it will remain 20 minutes late forever. RailVue AI replaces this with a Machine Learning engine that predicts delay deviation based on speed, remaining distance, track section congestion, weather, and historical corridor averages.

1. The Complete End-to-End Data Journey

When a passenger searches for Train 12019 (Howrah to Ranchi Shatabdi Express), here is how data moves through the codebase:

Stage Component What Happens Behind the Scenes

2. Backend API FastAPI (app/api/trains.py) Queries curated SQLite directory database of 1,500+ Indian Railway trains, loading full route halts and station milestones.
3. Location Tracking live_location_engine.py Matches distance covered (e.g. 280 of 436 km), identifies active segment (Bokaro → Ranchi), and sets station states (DEPARTED, APPROACHING, UPCOMING).

6. Physics Validation validation_layer.py Ensures maximum speed (130 km/h) is not violated and station ETAs remain in strictly increasing chronological order.
7. Passenger Screen TrainDetailsView.tsx Displays dynamic ETA (13:37), confidence score (95%), telemetry provenance badge, and explainable delay factors.

3. Weather & Congestion: How We Handled Missing Data

Standard railway logs (IRCTC/Kaggle) never contain weather or traffic telemetry. We solved this through Multi-Source Data Fusion and Derived Feature Engineering:

  • Weather Data Fusion: We linked external meteorological records (Open-Meteo) containing rainfall in mm, humidity, and winter fog visibility. Merged on relational key (station_code, date) in train_eta_pipeline.py:49. Rainfall >30 mm or fog visibility <200 m generates caution order speed drops.
  • Track Congestion Derivation: Congestion is an engineered spatial-temporal feature derived from train logs. For every route segment (e.g. CNB_PRYJ), hour, and day of week:
    Congestion Score = 0.35 × (Track Density) + 0.40 × (Average Delay) + 0.25 × (Speed Drop).
    Merged on relational key (route_segment, hour, day_of_week).

4. Inside the 80 XGBoost Trees

XGBoost builds trees sequentially as a mistake-correcting relay team:

  • Tree 1: Makes a coarse initial guess using remaining distance. Leaves an error (residual).
  • Tree 2: Specifically predicts and corrects the mistake of Tree 1 using congestion scores.
  • Tree 3 to 80: Progressively refine errors using weather disruptions, station dwell history, and time of day.
  • Why 80-150 Trees? 1 tree underfits (MAE ~24 min). 5,000 trees overfits and runs slowly. 80-150 trees with learning rate η = 0.03 hits the optimal sweet spot (MAE 10.37 min, inference under 15 milliseconds).

5. Model Benchmarks & Defending the R² = 0.99 Metric

How to Defend R² = 0.99: Total journey duration varies from 30 minutes to 1,200 minutes. Remaining distance alone accounts for 99% of total travel time variance—even a basic timetable formula gets R² = 0.9934!

The Real Machine Learning Test: When evaluated on pure delay deviation, the baseline has negative R², while our XGBoost model achieves R² = 0.4993 (~50%) and reduces arrival error from 26 min to 10.37 minutes!

7. Rapid Teacher Q&A Defense

Q: "Your R² is 0.99. Isn't that overfitted or leaking data?"
A: "No. R²=0.99 is on absolute travel time because distance naturally dominates trip duration. On pure delay deviation, our R² is 0.4993 (~50%), proving genuine predictive skill with zero target leakage under a journey-aware run split."
Q: "Where did weather and congestion come from if the dataset didn't have them?"
A: "We performed Multi-Source Data Fusion: joining Open-Meteo weather records on (station_code, date) and deriving congestion mathematically from active train density on (segment, hour, day)."
Q: "Why XGBoost over Deep Learning (LSTM)?"
A: "On tabular operational features (speed, distance, delays), gradient boosted trees outperform neural networks, require zero GPU infrastructure, and execute live predictions in under 15 milliseconds."
Q: "How do you prevent impossible physical predictions?"
A: "Our Physics & Sanity Validation Layer caps maximum speed at 130 km/h and strictly enforces increasing chronological station arrival times."
